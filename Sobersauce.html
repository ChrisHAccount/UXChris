Sobersauce — UX Case Study

This project demonstrates my ability to improve product findability, reduce checkout friction, and design for conversion.

Overview

Sobersauce is an eCommerce refreshment company that sells and delivers a range of low‑alcohol or alcohol‑free beverages. Analytics data revealed a high cart abandonment rate, negatively impacting sales. This case study explores how improving findability, clarity, and checkout flow can reduce friction and increase conversions.

Problem

Analytics data revealed a high cart abandonment rate, negatively impacting sales.

Users struggled to find products, understand page purpose, and complete checkout without confusion or frustration.

Research

Low findability resulted in high interaction costs.

The existing website structure included 19 separate product list pages with conflicting titles, such as:

  • 0.0% ABV Alcohol Free Beers
  • Alcohol Free Dark Beers
  • Alcohol Free IPAs
  • Non‑Alcohol Pale Ales

This inconsistency increased navigation steps and contributed to high drop‑off rates.

Insights

Feelings of impatience and frustration were experienced at checkout.

User interviews and walkthroughs revealed confusion, irritation, and difficulty locating product information. These findings aligned with analytics data.

Key opportunities:

  • Combine product categories and introduce sort/filter functionality.
  • Remove duplicate checkout information requests.
  • Signpost product information clearly.
  • Enable adding products to basket with minimal navigation.

Personas

Two primary user types emerged from interviews and analytics:

  • The Explorer — wants variety, filters, and clear comparisons.
  • The Decider — wants quick clarity, simple checkout, and reassurance.

User Journey

Journey mapping highlighted friction at product search, comparison, checkout, and delivery options.

Ideation

A popover contextual menu would present glanceable product information.

The popover appears when a product image is tapped, providing product details, quantity selection, and an “add to basket” CTA without leaving the results page.

The homepage and “Shop Beers” page were redesigned to promote popular products and introduce sort/filter functionality.

Wireframes

Iteration

Based on testing, checkout steps were reduced, product cards redesigned, navigation simplified, and visual hierarchy improved.

Usability Testing

Users stopped reporting feelings of confusion when finding products or checking out.

Think‑aloud testing showed users instinctively used search and filter functions. Feedback requested auto‑complete search due to creative drink naming.

Prototype

Outcome

  • Reduced checkout time
  • Improved product discoverability
  • Higher user satisfaction
  • Elimination of confusion during key tasks

Reflection

This project demonstrated how structural clarity, simplified flows, and contextual information can significantly improve user experience in product discovery.
